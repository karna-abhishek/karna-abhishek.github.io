Frustrated Quantum Spin Systems

Duke University Supervisor: Prof. Shailesh Chandrasekharan May 2024 - Present

Overview: The Sign Problem and PDMS

Quantum Monte Carlo methods are powerful tools for studying quantum many-body systems, but frustrated models—systems with competing correlations that prevent simultaneous minimization of local interactions—generically suffer from the notorious "sign problem," where cancellations of positive and negative weights prevent reliable sampling. The general form of the sign problem is NP-hard. Even in systems where ground-state calculations are possible, obtaining excited states stably remains challenging.

I developed a new Monte Carlo approach for numerical spectroscopy of generic lattice Hamiltonians, called the Projected Density Matrix Sampling (PDMS) method. By providing smooth and rapid convergence, PDMS accesses the excited spectrum easily in sign-free systems and remains fully robust at low temperatures in systems with sign problems. Instead of tracing the thermal density matrix ρ = e-βH over the full Hilbert space, PDMS judiciously samples in a much smaller projection subspace while maintaining ergodicity and detailed balance, still obtaining the correct answer. This work led to a first-author paper, now available on arXiv.

Theoretical Work

  • Analyzed the established ground state derivation for certain parameter ranges of the Shastry-Sutherland model (SSM), a geometrically frustrated 2D Heisenberg anti-ferromagnet
  • Applied theory of angular momentum and addition of spins to a 16-spin lattice to perform dimensionality reduction from 216 to 12,870 states for numerical ground state search in any parameter range
  • Formulated symmetry arguments to calculate total spin s for each energy state to recover the complete 216 states
  • Performed trotterization of the Boltzmann factor in the partition function for Quantum Monte Carlo (QMC)
  • Mapped energy search problem as a sampling problem through path integral formulation of quantum mechanics
  • Performed analytical calculations following ergodicity rules and detailed balance principle from stochastic process theory to understand constraints on the SSM Hamiltonian for algorithm development

Numerical Modeling and Results

  • Devised first-principles framework for Exact Diagonalization (ED) on 16-spin lattice SSM Hamiltonian
  • Performed ground state search for different coupling ratios and calculated S² matrices and total spin through diagonalization of all irreducible representations
  • Developed the new Projected Density Matrix Sampling (PDMS) method and solved 2-, 3-, 4-, 16-, 64-, and 96-site systems accurately
  • In the quantum Heisenberg limit (frustration removed), demonstrated very rapid convergence to obtain lowest-few eigenenergies
  • In frustrated regimes, showed complete agreement within errors with exact diagonalization results on small lattices
  • With collaborators at Penn State, applied PDMS to the sign-free 1D-critical Transverse Field Ising Model (TFIM) with longitudinal field, demonstrating the flow from the conformal limit to the massive E₈ quantum field theory by obtaining scaling relations and energies previously inaccessible to Monte Carlo methods
  • Presented results at the International Conference on Strongly Correlated Quantum Materials (ICSCQM 2025) in Hvar, Croatia, where discussions inspired experiment-informed projection strategies now being incorporated into the PDMS framework
  • Currently investigating algebraic lattice symmetries, "sign problem" in new projection subspaces, and multi-level ergodic sampling to improve the method's applicability
