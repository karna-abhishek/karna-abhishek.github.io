Research

Machine learning for quantum materials

Princeton University, 2026 to present. With B. Andrei Bernevig.

I build generative models for crystal structures and models that predict magnetic, superconducting and other properties of materials.

Quantum Monte Carlo for lattice Hamiltonians

Duke University, 2024 to 2026. With Shailesh Chandrasekharan.

I developed projected density matrix sampling, a continuous-time quantum Monte Carlo method for the low-lying spectrum of lattice Hamiltonians, free of Trotter error. We applied it to the Ising chain near the E8 critical point and to the frustrated Shastry–Sutherland model.

Physical Review D arXiv Poster, ICSCQM 2025 Slides, Max Planck Graduate Center 2026

Synthesis of quantum magnets

Duke University, 2022 to 2026. With Sara Haravifard.

I made the doped spinels LixZn1−xV2O4 and Cr-doped ZnAl2O4 by solid-state reaction and refined their structures from powder X-ray diffraction. I arc-melted the borides TmB4 and HoB4, grew single crystals of NdFeO3 in an optical floating-zone furnace, oriented them by Laue diffraction and measured their magnetization.

Poster, APS 2025 Poster, 2024 Poster, 2023

Air pollution in Nepal

Duke University, 2022 to 2026. With Brian McAdoo, PlanetLab.

We measured the transport of fine particulate matter across the border into the Terai of Nepal with a low-cost sensor network and back-trajectory modeling.

Atmospheric Pollution Research

Track reconstruction for the HL-LHC

Duke University, 2023. With Ashutosh Kotwal.

As a Huang Fellow I wrote algorithms that divide the silicon detector into wedges and sort collision hits into them, for parallel track reconstruction in the trigger.
