About

I'm a PhD student in Theoretical Physics at Princeton, working on quantum condensed matter. My current research applies machine learning to magnetic materials. I use it to classify, predict, and screen for properties that are hard to compute from first principles alone.

Before Princeton, I spent four years at Duke doing a bit of everything: building new Monte Carlo algorithms for quantum spin systems, growing single crystals in a furnace, and modeling pollution above the Himalayas. I also picked up a PPE certificate, wrote a poetry collection about fungi, and documented temples across South Asia and Southeast Asia. The common thread, I think, is that I find almost everything interesting.

Projected Density Matrix Sampling for Lattice Hamiltonians

Abhishek Karna, Hansen S. Wu, Shailesh Chandrasekharan, Ribhu K. Kaul

arXiv:2511.19209  ·  April 2026

We present a continuous-time path-integral Monte Carlo method for computing the low-lying spectrum of generic quantum Hamiltonians within a projection subspace. The method is free of Trotter discretization errors and converges systematically to low-energy states with finite overlap with the projection subspace as β increases. Applied to the 1D Ising model near the E₈ critical point and the frustrated Shastry–Sutherland model, demonstrating quantum Monte Carlo spectroscopy for lattice Hamiltonians.
